# Code and co-op

Part of the [game design](game_design.md). The folders under `src/` match these notes: world, player, enemy, combat, session, ui, and audio.

Definitions sit with the capability they describe. The Gunslinger's pace, hull, and revolver live in `src/player/`. A rifle, the squad's cover gait, and the shared backup live with gunner behavior. The zap-bot owns its jolt, its plate, and its spawn schedule. The grenade walker owns its launcher and its shield. Hit points, speeds, radii, and timings in those definitions are plain integers. 1000 units is 1 metre and 1 second. Factors, angles, muzzle offsets, and frame deltas stay floating-point. `units` is the step from a definition into a world transform or the frame clock, which still move in metres and seconds. Purchased levels derive player statistics from the Gunslinger definitions, and a spawn copies the hull, the pace, and the weapon onto the entity. Projectiles carry their own speed and hit radius from the firing weapon, so later weapons can vary these properties without changing shared projectile movement. Every damageable body carries a `HitCircle`, and one walk tests that circle. Keep live timers, rounds, health, and AI state on entities. When the Mage and the Steamist are built, their fuel, pressure, patch, and canister state belong on the weapon entity the same way the Gunslinger's drums do. Do not put that state into the shared session.

Use small focused Bevy systems and components for player intent, movement, health, enemies, projectiles, cover, and encounter state. A hit is a `Struck` message: which body, what the round is made of, whether it is kinetic or energy, and how much damage it carries. The body lists `Abilities` in order. Each ability changes that hit or stops the chain, then sends a `Notice` for the hurt, the death, the impact, or a layer that spent part of the hit. A grenade blast is a `Struck` whose material is explosive, so the allied-shot stop lets it through and every body in the circle handles it. `HitPoints` or `PlayerLife`, with a `Health` value, is what makes a body destructible. `ReduceDamage` and `EnergyShield` can sit ahead of those. Sounds, orbs, and removal follow the notice. `PlayerIntent` records desired movement, world-space aim, and whether the fire control is held. The keyboard/mouse adapter writes that intent before gameplay runs; movement, weapons, and animation then apply the game's rules. `KeyboardMouseControlled` marks the input source, `LocalPlayer` marks the camera and HUD subject, and `PlayerSlot` gives a stable in-run target for another input adapter. A future gamepad, network, screen-share, or MCP bridge can feed the same intent for its assigned slot without reading devices inside gameplay systems. An ordinary screen-share keyboard/mouse stream controls the existing local slot; a second simultaneous player needs a separate input channel. Enemy attack timing and group behavior live on the relevant entities. Keep the rendering/model setup separate from combat state.

The Steamist's landing cross is the point on the mouse aim where the canister would land if the button were released now. Compute it from that aim and the current pressure, and launch to the same point. Do not read the mouse a second time.

Networking is not implemented. The eventual multiplayer model is networked co-op, not multiple people on one keyboard. Gameplay movement, aiming, shooting, and enemy targeting already iterate over players. Co-op still needs decisions and implementation for camera framing, per-player HUD and shop selection, checkpoint crossing, death/revival, and player-to-player spacing. The current menus use a single-player shop and the phase can end when any player crosses the checkpoint; do not treat those as finished co-op rules.
